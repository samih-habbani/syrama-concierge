Every May, the Principality does something no other Formula 1 venue can: it turns its own harbour into the best seat in the sport. The cars run within a few metres of the water, the yachts moor stern-to along the barrier, and for four days Port Hercule is both paddock overflow and the most exclusive grandstand in motorsport.

Getting a client into that picture is almost entirely a logistics problem, and it starts months early.

## The berth is the ticket

Hotel rooms in Monaco during Grand Prix week are booked a year out and priced accordingly. The more reliable move — and the better one — is to arrive by sea. A [crewed charter yacht](/yachting) berthed in Port Hercule gives you a private base with a view of the swimming-pool section, catering that never queues, and somewhere to host that isn't a restaurant fighting the same crowd.

Berths inside the port are allocated through the Yacht Club de Monaco and a small number of agents, and they move fast. By March the good ones are gone. If a client commits late, the fallback is a berth just outside the harbour with a tender running guests in — workable, but you feel the difference.

## What a day actually looks like

- **Morning:** breakfast aboard, then Paddock Club transfer on foot or by tender depending on the berth.
- **Track running:** Paddock Club terraces over the pit lane, with a pit-lane walk on the Thursday and Saturday.
- **Afternoon:** back to the yacht as the crowds thicken. This is the part guests underestimate — the ability to step out of 100,000 people and into a quiet deck is the whole point.
- **Evening:** the Amber Lounge and the brand parties, or a private dinner aboard with drivers and team principals when we can arrange it.

## The details that go wrong

Two things catch people out. The first is ground movement: central Monaco closes to cars, so a helicopter transfer from Nice — a seven-minute flight — is not a luxury, it is the only sensible way in and out on race day. We block those slots when we block the berth.

The second is the guest list. A yacht that sleeps eight will host thirty across the weekend, and every one of them needs Paddock Club credentials in their own name, collected in advance. We handle that centrally so nobody is standing at a will-call window on Sunday morning.

Monaco is the clearest example of something we say a lot: [the event is only as good as the machinery around it](/blog/the-two-hour-jet). Get the berth, the helicopter and the credentials right, and the racing takes care of itself.
